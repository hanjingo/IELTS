Q: Should schools teach both arts and science?

A: Yes, schools should teach both arts and science. While science education is essential for developing critical thinking and problem-solving skills, arts education is equally important for fostering creativity, self-expression, and cultural awareness. Incorporation of both arts and science education into the school curriculum can help students develop a more holistic understanding of the world and prepare them for diverse career paths. Additionally, exposure to the arts can improve students' mental health, empathy, and overall well-being.

Q: What kinds of courses are useful for university students?

A: University students can benefit from various courses, including those directly related to their major, as well as courses in other areas. Courses in their major provide them with the necessary knowledge and skills to succeed in their chosen career field. Additionally, courses in other areas, such as critical thinking, communication, and problem-solving, can help them develop transferable skills that employers highly value. Courses that expose students to different cultures, languages, and perspectives can also broaden their horizons and prepare them to work in a globalized world.

Q: Why do some students dislike studying at school?

A: There are several reasons why some students dislike studying at school. One of the most common reasons is a lack of interest in the subject matter. Students may find some subjects boring or irrelevant to their interests or career goals. Additionally, external factors such as family problems, mental health issues, or socioeconomic status can also affect students' attitudes toward studying.
