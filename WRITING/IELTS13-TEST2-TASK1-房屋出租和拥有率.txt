The bar chart illustrates the proportion of households living in owned and rented accommodation in England and Wales from 1918 to 2011.

Overall, the general trend over this period shows a significant increase in homeownership, while the proportion of rented accommodation has correspondingly decreased. However, there were some fluctuations within this trend.

In 1918, the vast majority of households (around 78%) were in rented accommodation, with only about 22% owning their homes. This pattern remained relatively stable through the following decades, with slight changes in 1939 and 1953, when the percentage of rented accommodation was approximately 68% and 69% respectively.

Starting from 1961, a notable shift began, with the percentage of homeownership rising steadily. By 1971, the gap between rented and owned accommodation had narrowed significantly, with about 50% of households owning their homes and 50% renting. This marked the first time that the proportions were equal.

The trend towards homeownership continued to grow, peaking in 2001 when approximately 69% of households were homeowners compared to 31% renting. However, by 2011, this trend saw a slight reversal, with the percentage of owned households decreasing to 64% and rented households increasing to 36%.

In conclusion, from 1918 to 2011, there was a clear overall trend of increasing homeownership in England and Wales, with a significant rise from the mid-20th century onwards. Despite this long-term trend, the most recent data from 2011 indicates a slight move back towards renting.
