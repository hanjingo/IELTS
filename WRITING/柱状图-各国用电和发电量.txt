The bar chart illustrates the electricity production and consumption in the top ten countries in 2014, measured in billion kWh.

Overall, China and the United States were by far the largest producers and consumers of electricity, significantly outpacing the other eight countries listed. Notably, most countries had electricity production levels closely matching their consumption, with only Germany producing considerably more electricity than it consumed.

China topped the list with 5,398 billion kWh of electricity produced and 5,322 billion kWh consumed. The United States followed, producing 4,099 billion kWh and consuming 3,866 billion kWh. These figures show that China's production was nearly 1,300 billion kWh more than that of the United States, and its consumption was approximately 1,456 billion kWh higher.

Russia ranked third with 1,057 billion kWh produced and 1,038 billion kWh consumed. Following Russia, Japan produced 934.2 billion kWh and consumed 856.7 billion kWh. India's production was 871 billion kWh, with consumption at 698.8 billion kWh, placing it fifth in both categories.

The remaining countries, including Canada, France, Brazil, Germany, and the Republic of Korea, had production and consumption figures ranging from around 485 billion kWh to 618 billion kWh. Among these, Germany was unique, with its production (526.6 billion kWh) significantly exceeding its consumption (582.5 billion kWh).

In conclusion, China and the United States dominated global electricity production and consumption in 2014, with both countries producing and consuming far more electricity than the other nations in the top ten. Most countries produced amounts of electricity that were closely aligned with their consumption, highlighting the importance of balancing supply and demand.
