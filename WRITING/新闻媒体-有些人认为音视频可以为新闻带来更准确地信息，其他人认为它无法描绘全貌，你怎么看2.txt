Some people hold the view that visual images can precisely depict the information and can communicate it more effectively, whereas others argue that they cannot present the full story. In this essay, we will explore both views and come to a conclusion.

They can show the emotions of the people involved, add depth to the story, and make the audience feel connected to the news. For instance, a powerful image of a natural disaster can grab the audience's attention and communicate the severity of the situation more effectively than a lengthy article.

However, some people argue that visual images do not necessarily represent the full story. They say that images could be manipulated or biased to convey particular viewpoints. In some circumstances, images alone can be misleading, and it is essential to read the complementary text to understand the context fully.

In my opinion, visual images are an essential aspect of a news story, but they should be combined with other forms of information to provide a comprehensive understanding. A video or photograph may tell a story visually, but it cannot depict the whole story. Text can provide necessary explanations, background information, different contexts, and multiple perspectives to reduce the risk of misunderstandings or misunderstandings. In this way, the audience can grasp the news story correctly.

In conclusion, while visual images are unquestionably critical, they are not enough to represent the entire news story. They need to be supplemented with appropriate, unbiased text to provide a more comprehensive understanding for the audience.
